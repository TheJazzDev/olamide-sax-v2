The Sound

Artistic Practice

African Contemporary at its heart — a warm, improvisational Afro-fusion that carries African rhythm into soulful melody. Here is the sound, the genres and the rooms it is made for.

01 The Sound

Four voices in one performance.

Olamide’s African Contemporary sound is built from four elements held in balance — the horn, the voice, the keys and the drum. Together they make an Afro-fusion that is at once rooted and modern.

Rich saxophone melodies
The horn carries the breath — soaring, warm-toned lines at the centre of every set.
Soulful vocals
A vocalist first — melody, call-and-response and the griot’s storytelling brought into the room.
Dynamic keyboard
The harmonic foundation — chords, texture and live improvisation binding the movements together.
Authentic African rhythms
The pulse of home beneath it all — Yoruba percussion keeping time under every phrase.

02 Genres

Where the sound lives.

Primary

African Contemporary

The heart of the practice — a modern African sound that fuses saxophone, voice and keys with authentic rhythm. Improvisational, warm and built for a live room, it moves easily between the intimate and the ceremonial.

Also

Gospel & Instrumental Worship

A dedicated worship register — instrumental gospel led by the saxophone, with keys and voice shaping reflective, spirit-led sets for churches and services. The same breath, turned toward worship.

03 Repertoire

A set, shaped to the room.

Every set is arranged for its occasion — Afro-fusion originals and reinterpreted favourites, soulful ballads, upbeat groove and instrumental worship. Whether the room asks for celebration, reflection or ceremony, the repertoire bends to meet it, with live improvisation keeping each performance one of a kind.

04 Participatory Arts

More than performance.

Every performance begins with music and ends with connection. As a participatory artist and creative facilitator, Olamide creates workshops that combine music, wellbeing, storytelling, memory work and community participation — inviting people not only to listen, but to take part and belong. He collaborates with leading UK cultural organisations, including Stand & Be Counted Theatre, darts & Maya Productions and ArtBomb UK, and has led recent work from the record-breaking World Record Football Scarf Procession to the Eco Art Fashion Show and Sharrow Festival.
