## Tone rules this draft follows (why it reads "flat")

Wikipedia rejects promotional writing. So this draft deliberately:
- States only facts that a **cited, independent source** supports.
- Uses no adjectives like "talented", "renowned", "acclaimed".
- Attributes opinions ("described by *The Guardian* as…") rather than asserting them.
- Omits anything sourced only to his own website or social media (those don't
  count toward notability and read as promotional).

## Draft article (plain text)

**Olamide Phillips Olaniyan** (born in Nigeria), known professionally as
**Olamide Sax**, is a Nigerian-born, United Kingdom–based multi-instrumentalist
and participatory artist. His work spans Afro-fusion music, live performance,
theatre and community arts.

### Career

Olaniyan began in traditional Yoruba percussion before performing on saxophone,
keyboard and vocals. Based in Yorkshire, England, he has performed at UK venues
and festivals including CAST Theatre and Stand & Be Counted Theatre in
Doncaster and Sheffield.[1][2]

In 2026 he performed with Stand & Be Counted Theatre during a Guinness World
Record attempt for the longest football scarf, held in Sheffield; the event was
reported by the BBC.[3] The same year, *The Guardian* published critical reviews
of his performance in the stage production *ÀTÙPÀ (The Spotlight)* at CAST,
Doncaster, and of his participatory work at Collective Routes & Beyond.[1][2]
His performance of his original work "Mama" at Stand & Be Counted Theatre's
Soapbox Spotlight in Sheffield was covered by *The Star*.[4]

He is a graduate of the darts and Maya Productions Artist Development Programme
in creative health and participatory arts, and is the founder of Lammy Wonder
Music Entertainment.[5]
